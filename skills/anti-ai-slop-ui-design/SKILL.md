## Purpose

This skill teaches how to recognize and avoid common “AI slop” patterns in UI/UX design, typography, layout, motion, and copywriting.
These patterns are strongly associated with low-quality autogenerated interfaces, generic SaaS templates, and visually noisy AI-generated products.
The goal is not minimalism for its own sake — it is intentionality, readability, hierarchy, and visual confidence.

---

# Core Principle

Avoid decorative decisions that exist only to signal “modern design.”

Every visual treatment must solve a real problem:

* hierarchy
* grouping
* affordance
* emphasis
* readability
* branding
* navigation
* interaction clarity

If a design element exists purely because “AI UIs usually have it,” remove it.

---

# Surface & Card Design

## Avoid: Border accent on rounded element

Thick accent borders on rounded cards clash with curved geometry and feel autogenerated.

### Better

* Use subtle borders
* Reduce radius
* Use background contrast instead of borders

---

## Avoid: Glassmorphism everywhere

Blurred glass cards, glow borders, and translucent layers should not be decorative defaults.

### Better

* Use solid surfaces
* Reserve blur for overlays/modals
* Create hierarchy with spacing and typography instead

---

## Avoid: Side-tab accent border

Colored stripes on the left edge of cards are one of the most recognizable AI-generated UI tells.

### Better

* Use subtle icons
* Use spacing and typography for emphasis
* Use soft background tinting if needed

---

## Avoid: Hairline border with wide shadow

Combining a 1px border with a giant soft shadow creates muddy depth.

### Better

Choose one:

* crisp edge
* soft elevation

Not both.

---

## Avoid: Repeating-gradient stripes

Decorative stripe textures are filler, not design.

### Better

* Flat surfaces
* Intentional texture
* Real imagery

---

## Avoid: Extreme border-radius on cards

Over-rounding everything creates blob interfaces.

### Better

* Cards: 8–16px radius
* Pills only for chips/buttons/tags

---

## Avoid: Nested cards

Cards inside cards create unnecessary depth and clutter.

### Better

* Use spacing
* Use dividers
* Flatten hierarchy

---

# Typography

## Avoid: Flat type hierarchy

When headings and body text are nearly the same size, interfaces feel unfinished.

### Better

Maintain strong scale contrast:

* heading
* subheading
* body
* caption

---

## Avoid: Icon tile stacked above heading

The “rounded icon square above title” layout is an AI SaaS template cliché.

### Better

* Inline icons
* Side-by-side layouts
* Icons without containers

---

## Avoid: Italic serif display headline

Oversized italic serif headlines are massively overused in AI startup landing pages.

### Better

* Roman serif
* Distinctive sans-serif
* Editorial typography only when context supports it

---

## Avoid: Hero eyebrow / pill chip

Tiny uppercase labels above hero headlines feel templated.

### Better

* Integrate context into headline
* Use breadcrumbs
* Use real navigation structure

---

## Avoid: Repeated section kicker labels

Repeating tiny uppercase labels above every section becomes scaffolding noise.

### Better

Create structure using:

* spacing
* imagery
* layout
* rhythm

---

## Avoid: Oversized hero headline

Huge paragraph-length hero text destroys hierarchy.

### Better

* Large short headline
* Smaller long headline
* Tight concise messaging

---

## Avoid: Crushed letter spacing

Excessively tight tracking hurts readability.

---

## Avoid: Overused font choices

Fonts like Inter, Geist, Space Grotesk, and Instrument Serif are heavily overused in AI-generated UIs.

### Better

Choose typography with:

* personality
* context
* uniqueness
* readability

---

## Avoid: Single font for everything

Using one typeface for all elements flattens hierarchy.

### Better

Combine:

* display font
* body font

---

## Avoid: All-caps body text

Long uppercase text reduces readability.

### Better

Reserve uppercase for:

* labels
* badges
* short headings

---

## Avoid: Wide letter spacing on body text

Wide tracking slows reading.

---

## Avoid: Tight line height

Dense paragraphs are hard to scan.

### Better

Body text should typically use:

* 1.5–1.7 line height

---

## Avoid: Tiny body text

Small text feels hostile and inaccessible.

### Better

* Minimum ~14px body text
* Prefer 16px

---

# Color & Contrast

## Avoid: AI color palette

Purple gradients and cyan-on-dark palettes are immediate AI tells.

### Better

Build deliberate palettes from:

* brand identity
* mood
* content type
* audience

---

## Avoid: Dark mode with glowing accents

Neon glows are the default “AI cyberpunk” aesthetic.

### Better

* Subtle shadows
* Soft lighting
* Controlled accents

---

## Avoid: Gradient text

Gradient-filled text hurts readability.

### Better

Use solid text colors.

---

## Avoid: Cream / beige palette by default

Warm beige surfaces are often chosen reflexively to appear “tasteful.”

### Better

Choose backgrounds intentionally.

---

## Avoid: Low contrast text

Light gray on white fails readability and accessibility.

### Better

Meet WCAG AA standards.

---

# Layout & Spacing

## Avoid: Hero metric layout

“10M+ users”, “99.9% uptime”, “200ms latency” layouts feel generic and untrustworthy.

---

## Avoid: Identical card grids

Infinite repeated feature cards are a hallmark of generated landing pages.

### Better

Introduce variation:

* asymmetry
* media
* layout changes
* visual rhythm

---

## Avoid: Monotonous spacing

Using the same spacing value everywhere kills rhythm.

### Better

Use:

* tight related grouping
* generous section separation

---

## Avoid: Numbered section markers

“01 / 02 / 03” section labels feel like autogenerated editorial scaffolding.

### Better

Use numbering only when content is genuinely sequential.

---

## Avoid: Cramped padding

Text should not touch container edges.

### Better

Use:

* minimum 8px padding
* ideally 12–16px

---

## Avoid: Body text touching viewport edge

Paragraphs flush against screen edges feel unfinished.

### Better

Wrap content inside containers with horizontal padding.

---

## Avoid: Line length too long

Very wide paragraphs are hard to read.

### Better

Limit body text width:

* 65ch–75ch

---

# Motion

## Avoid: Bounce or elastic easing

Overshooting animations feel tacky in normal interfaces.

### Better

Use:

* ease-out
* quart
* quint
* expo

---

## Avoid: Layout property animation

Animating width/height causes layout jank.

### Better

Animate:

* transform
* opacity

---

## Avoid: Image hover transform

Scaling images on hover is an overused generated-UI interaction.

### Better

Keep imagery stable or use subtle interaction feedback.

---

# Copywriting

## Avoid: Em-dash overuse

Excessive em-dashes create stereotypical AI cadence.

---

## Avoid: Marketing buzzwords

Generic SaaS language destroys credibility.

### Better

Describe literally what the product does.

---

## Avoid: Aphoristic-cadence copy

Repeated “Not X. Y.” phrasing feels machine-generated.

### Better

Use direct statements.

---

## Avoid: Theater framing copy

Calling things “theater” or “performative” is a recurring AI writing tic.

### Better

State clearly:

* what works
* what doesn’t
* why

---

# General Quality

## Avoid: Justified text

Web browsers produce uneven spacing without advanced hyphenation.

### Better

Use left-aligned body text.

---

## Avoid: Skipped heading levels

Heading hierarchy matters for accessibility and structure.

### Better

Use proper semantic progression:

* h1
* h2
* h3

---

## Avoid: Broken or placeholder images

Empty image tags destroy perceived quality.

### Better

Use:

* real assets
* generated assets
* no image at all

---

# Final Guideline

Good interfaces feel intentional.

AI slop happens when:

* decoration replaces structure
* trends replace decisions
* effects replace hierarchy
* templates replace design

The solution is not “less style.”
The solution is deliberate style.
